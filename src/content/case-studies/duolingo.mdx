###### Overview
## A new course using the same system.

This is a concept project inspired by my time tutoring high school biology

<DuolingoCharacter />
The sciences have a branding issue. They’re just not popular. Few students enjoy science classes. This is something I’ve witnessed firsthand while tutoring biology to high school students. Rote memorization and abstract concepts don't feel connected to everyday life can make learning any science subject uninteresting. Sadly, low engagement and interest have caused low science scores on the Regents exam, with only 44% of students achieving proficiency.

## Imagining biology in the app

###### Research
## From classroom to app

During my time tutoring, I noticed that it was difficult to get students to study. They did not find the material engaging or relatable. Although we would go over and discuss topics in the classroom, I noticed that the students ahd trouble explainging or applying what we discussed in after-school sessions. 

Hence, I pivoted. I started to connect what they were learning to something they might experience in real-life. Doing this changed the way students understood a topic. As a result, they did better on tests. 

Student pain points:
- Unengaging content
- Confusing explanations and a lot of memorizations
- Abstract concepts and little visualization and connection to real - life.
- Stuggling to apply concepts

#### Competitive analysis
There are many apps available that help students memorize and study various topics. While many of them are usefull tools, they fall short. They don't help students make that connection between learning and applying.

My idea for a biology course in Duolingo aims to do just using the tools that exist in the app already (i.e. DuoRadio, Duo Stories, etc). 

###### Process
## A challenge on innovation and restraint

I didn’t set out to invent something new. I set out to borrow and extend something that works, the Duolingo app,  to something that wasn’t, science education. Duolingo has already successfully expanded to cover Math, Chess, and Music. Why not science? Each subject creates a unique experience and ensures that learning is not static. Whether it's by playing along to popular pop songs or having chess matches with Oscar, each engagement encourages users to apply the skills they have learned. The same can be done for biology. One of the challenges students face is applying their knowledge to graphs and models. But Duolingo can help. 

[insert image]

## Creating a new biology learning experience
Part of designing this course was looking at the current curriculum, specifically New York State’s curriculum, and coming up with ways to inject delight into the experience. One setback of the current curriculum was relating material and creating connections between each topic. With Life, Explained, I took Duolingo’s classic learning path structure to create lessons that cascade into each other, connections from one sub-topic to another.  This new structure allows students to see the relevance between each concept and follow a through line that helps them make sense of biology as a whole, connecting one concept to another. For example, the first learning section may be about cell structure. In this topic, the student learns about cell structure, osmosis, organelles, and the function of each organelle. The following lesson might focus on organs and organ systems, offering first a micro view to then expand to macro. This progression traces out the way each concept relates to others, hopefully helping students see the connections between learned concepts and real life.

###### Final Design
### Defining metrics
[insert video walk through]
Although this is a concept project, if it shipped, success would look like students returning to biology the same way they return to Spanish: habitually, because something that once felt irrelevant now feels personal. Daily active users and lesson completion rates within science units would be the primary signals. 

###### Reflection
### Micro and macro views
Working on Life, Explained pushed me to think at two levels simultaneously: big picture — what science education could look like inside Duolingo — and detail level — designing within an existing system for a specific audience while still making it feel personal.That tension between constraint and creativity is where the most interesting design problems live. And it's exactly the kind of problem I'd want to keep solving.‍
