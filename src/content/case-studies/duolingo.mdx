###### Discovery
## Imagining biology in the app 
Duolingo has already successfully expanded courses to include Math, Music and Chess. So, what if Duolingo could make learning science fun and worth caring about? What would that look like?

###### Research
## From classroom to app
During my time tutoring, I noticed that it was difficult to motivate students to study. Although the students understood the concepts in after-school sessions, they had difficulty applying concepts on exams.  

Hence, I pivoted. I began to connect biology concepts to real-life experiences.   Doing this changed the way students understood a topic. As a result, they did better on tests. 

Student pain points:
- Unengaging content
- Confusing explanations and a lot of memorizations
- Abstract concepts and little visualization and connection to real life.
- Struggling to apply concepts

My concept biology course for Duolingo aims to fill in gaps that do so just using the tools that exist in the app already (i.e., DuoRadio, Duo Stories, etc.).

###### Process
## A challenge on innovation and restraint

With this design, I leaned into Duolingo’s gamified engine and features and applied them to a course that was not offered. 

Duolingo has already successfully expanded to cover Math, Chess, and Music. Why not science? Each subject creates a unique experience and ensures that learning is not static. Whether it's learning the notes to popular pop songs or having chess matches with Oscar, each interaction converts learned knowledge into applied knowledge. 

The same can be done for biology. One of the challenges students face is applying their knowledge to graphs and models. But Duolingo can help. 


## Creating a new biology learning experience
Although I am familiar with New York State’s biology curriculum, I could not rely on it to design a learning experience. The new curriculum and standards fail students. There is a mismatch between in-class lessons, labs, and Regents exams and vague grading criteria. 

My goals were:

* Move students past memorization and into application. 
* Close learning gaps.
* Strengthen critical and analytical skills through interactive labs. 

An advantage of Duolingo’s learning path structure is that each topic connects. However, it did not cover all of my goals. 

#### Hands-on learning through features.

Lily AI: 
Duolingo has helped students apply their language learning skills through virtual conversations with Lily. What would that look like in a biology course?

![Lily asking about natural selection in a call](/media/case-studies/duolingo/lily-talking.webp)

At the end of a unit, students would discuss a specific topic, such as natural selection. Students are tested on how well they explain natural selection, helping cement concepts and lessons.  

![Biology in real life](/media/case-studies/duolingo/bio-in-real-life.png)

Duo Stories:
I’ve used Duo to learn Korean, and one of my favorite features is Duo Stories. Duo Stories help contextualize vocabulary within scenarios. Leveraging this feature could help students better understand terms. 

![Biology in real life](/media/case-studies/duolingo/bio-in-real-life.png)

DuoRadio:
In the biology course, Duo Radio takes a wider approach. Currently, Duolingo Radio helps learners identify words in conversations. However, in a biology course, it can expand concepts by providing in-depth discussion about scientific discoveries. For example, during a podcast hosted by Falstaff, students can learn more about food webs, or in Zari’s podcast, they can learn about genetic modification.  

![Junior's talk show](/media/case-studies/duolingo/duo-radio.webp)

AR lens: 
This would be a new feature for the course. The AR lens would help students see objects or animals in everyday life through the perspective of biology.

###### Final Design
### Defining metrics
Although this is a concept project, if shipped, success would look like students returning to biology the same way they return to Spanish: habitually, because something that once felt irrelevant now feels personal. Daily active users and lesson completion rates within science units would be the primary signals.

###### Reflection
### Micro and macro views
Working on Life, Explained pushed me to think at two levels simultaneously: big picture — what science education could look like inside Duolingo — and detail level — designing within an existing system for a specific audience while still making it feel personal. That tension between constraint and creativity is where the most interesting design problems live. And it's exactly the kind of problem I'd want to keep solving.‍
