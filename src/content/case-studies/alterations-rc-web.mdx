###### Overview

## Alterations by R.C.’s website was getting more than enough views, but not enough customers. 

When Alterations by R.C. initially opened, there was a lot of online buzz around the newly opened store. Site traffic was up and the inbox was full. However, none of those inquires and clicks were translating to real in-store customers.
### Solution
{/* Image link: https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6472406d34ccbfd57740801d/6a3edcb37522093e55e0f901_ALTERATIONS-%20WEB.gif */}

Drive potential customers to the store by presenting relevant information about business services above the the fold, optimizing the booking appointment flow, and tailoring the website to align with business values.

### Outcomes
After the changes to the website, in-store attendance rose 15%, bounce rate dropped by half and questions regarding pricing in the store’s inbox dropped completely. 

###### Discovery

## Poor layout and lack of visual identity were keeping customers away from the store.
 
 Navigation on the current site was confusing. Pages and relevant information where hidden inside a hamburger menu. This made it difficult for customers to access information about services and pricing, and subconsciously made it difficult for customers to trust in the business. 

![Page usability issues found via site analytics](/media/case-studies/alterations-rc-web/content/alterations-by-rc-web-analytics.png)

### There were many pain points on the homepage, but the primary issue was the site’s navigation.

![Highlighting usability issues on the homepage](/media/case-studies/alterations-rc-web/content/alterations-audit.png)

Although hamburger menus are popular in mobile site designs, this original site was built for desktop. Therefore, it had two issues. One, it did not have a fluid responsive design, which led to the second— a hidden and confusing menu. As a result, customers had issues navigating to other pages, primarily the pricing page. This issues was clear in the amount of inbox messages asking about pricing information. 

###### Research

## Reorganizing site architecture to make relevant information more visible.

Looking at the initial data, I assume that the site only had a discoverability issue, but a deeper look into the messages and bounce rates implied a findability issue as well.

![Comparing old and new informtaion architecture of site](/media/case-studies/alterations-rc-web/content/alterations-info-architecture.png)

Swapping the hamburger menu for a horizontal bar menu allowed the users to navigate freely from one page to the next, giving them access to information and removing doubt from their journey. ‍

###### Process

#### Explorations

In the redesign, I wanted to go beyound improving navigation. I wanted the users to be part of the process.

##### Exploration 1

![First design direction for new site](/media/case-studies/alterations-rc-web/content/alterations-first-design.png)

The first iteration of the design divided the services into menswear and womenswear, but this design added more pages— increasing the risk of page bloat. However, this iteration, with customer profiles, helped create ideate a customer facing self-service kiosk. Both of these ideas came from a necessity to speed up the alteration workflow for staff while finding ways to include customers into the alterations process. 

#### Exploration 2

![Second design direction for new site](/media/case-studies/alterations-rc-web/content/alterations-second-design.png)

The second iteration heavily relied on video and image content, an attempt to create a more engaging site. However, this layout did not make pricing easily accessible for customers. 

#### Testing and iterating

![Insights from A/B test](/media/case-studies/alterations-rc-web/content/alterations-ab-test-insights.png)

After running an A/B test for three weeks, more than 70% of site visitors moved past the homepage. Visibility for the booking flow increase in-store conversion by more than 30%. 

<VoiceNote src="/media/case-studies/alterations-rc-web/your-note.mp3" label="Finding the business's voice." />

## Changing colors and tone helped create a warmer and more honest site that turned site visitors into customers.

An A/B test demonstrated two key changes: 1. changing the color palette and tone of voice created more booking appointments and  2. moving popular services to the homepage instilled a sense of transparency towards the customers, which made them feel comfortable booking appointments. 

Key Insight : Customers were moved by an honest and friendly approach tone.

A new brand identity that showcased company voice.

![Visual identity for Alteration's new website](/media/case-studies/alterations-rc-web/content/alterations-visual-identity.png)

The final layout, colors and microcopy worked together to create visual identity that would later serve as the foundation for the business’s brand identity.

###### Reflection

## Go with your instincts.

When I began the redesign for the Alterations by R.C., I had many instinctual design choices that I often second guessed but later on ended up choosing them. This project taught me to trust my design instincts instead of doubting, it saves time and helps ship a finished product faster. ‍
