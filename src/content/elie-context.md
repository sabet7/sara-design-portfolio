[Who I am

Hello, My name is Sara Del Villar. I am a multidisciplinary designer with a focus on product design. I work across different platforms and concepts with the aim of connecting business to users through delightful and engaging experiences. 

My background isn't a straight line into design. I studied Film and Media Studies at Smith College (directing focus), worked in film production on a variety of television shows, and tutored students while between projects. I pivoted into product design during the pandemic. I think of my career as closing the gap between information and the people who need it.

During the pandemic and beyond, I have worked as a Founding Product Designer, leading and shipping 0 to 1 builds for small businesses. 

What I'm looking for

I'm job searching for a full-time product design role while freelancing and building independent products.  Currently, I’m building an EdTech application, inspired by my time tutoring biology. I work best in 0-to-1 SaaS projects and EdTech, since it’s a natural extension of my background. But, I am open to working across industries. 

How I work

I've mostly worked as a solo designer , which means that I own the full process — research, design, build, and iteration — rather than handing off between specialists. This is just a structural fact of freelancing, but it does not in an way demonstarate a gap in my ability to collaborate. More than happy to work with a group of like minded creatives. 

I use AI tools as part of my actual design and build workflow, not just for polish — Claude (Code and Chat) for development and iteration, plus tools like Lovable and Gemini depending on the project. My case studies document this explicitly because it's how I actually work now. 

Although I like to use AI to protoype, my design process begins analog— with pen and paper. The start of a new design is always my favorite part, learning about the user and the problem before brainstorming different solutions. 

I'm careful about honesty in how I represent my work: I don't inflate a hackathon prototype into a shipped product, or call something "SaaS" before it has paying customers. If a claim isn't interview-defensible, I don't make it.
