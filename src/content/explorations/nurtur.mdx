###### Summary

This design was created for Google’s DeepMind Gemini Hackathon in Feb 2026.

## Finding healthy groceries in food desert regions is a challenge because many of the options are fast food.
There are many apps that deliver food straight to your door, but few that address the real issue of  “Where should I get food?” This difficult question is a reality for individuals living in food deserts. Food deserts are characterized by a lack of a nearby supermarket and also by chains of fast food stores that don’t offer healthy options for local residents. Therefore, the problem is not ordering food, but rather healthy alternatives.

##### Discovery
SInce this project was to be completed within 48 hours, I had minimal time to do research. My starting point was my pwn community. A few blocks streets away from my home, a supermarket burned down. While it has since been re-built, I couldn't help think about those who do not have  easy access to groceries. 

In my research, I learned that 6% of Americans, approximately 18.8 - 19 million people, live in food deserts, and yet there are 60 to 103 million tons of food are thrown out each year. How do I fix that?

For the hackathon, I set out to fix food waste and food insecurity through a food sourcing app that connect users, to farmers, groceries, and other suppliers— helping them feed themselves and also helping farmer's sell their "ugly" produce.

## Tackling two problems at once
Food scarcity is an access problem while food waste is a supply and distribution problem. In America, food deserts are largely created by three major factors:  physical access, caused by transportation or zoning laws and real-estate, and economics. Supermakret chains feel discouraged in setting up in low-incmoe neighborhoods due to low purchasing power. 

Sadly, for users the journey to find healthy groceries was difficult. 

![User journey](/media/explorations/nurtur/content/nurtur-customer-journey.png)

My app focused on tackling access. How can I make healthier food more accessible?

The on-boarding process of the app would be key. During the on-boarding process, the app asks users for their names, dietary restrictions and location. This data is used to narrow and focus results that meet the users's needs.

### Connecting farmers to customers
For this app to be effective, it needed to also allow farmers to sell their goods. On the other side of access were farmers. When creating this solution, I took NYC as an example. Farmer's markets appear all over the city, however, there are pockets of food scarce regions thorughout the five boroughs. Some farms offer drop off stops for their goods and CSA programs with grocery boxes shipped to your home, but few New Yorkers know about these programs. How can I make people aware of these options?

![Farmer journey](/media/explorations/nurtur/content/nurtur-farmer-journey.png)

In NYC, there are various steps a grower must go through to sell at a farmer's market. It's a long process with time constraints and bureaucratic hurdles. Nurtur handles the process easily, connecting farmers with verified accounts to sell after the receive approval.

## Making the most of what is available
Another way of tackling food waste was through a the recipe feature. The recipe feature is a scanner. The scan looks through the user's fridge and suggests healthy meals with the ingredients they already have.
